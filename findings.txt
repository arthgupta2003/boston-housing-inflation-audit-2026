Does inflation exaggerate the Greater Boston renter statistic?
Analysis date: November 18, 2026

RESULT

Yes, if it is read as a comparison of households at the same real income.
Using downloaded ACS household records, nominal $35,000-$49,999 incomes
produce 9.4% severely burdened in 2006 and 50.9% in 2024,
rounding to the report's 9% and 51%. Adjusting the 2024 bracket to
$54,459.90-$77,799.85 produces 21.5%, an increase of 12.1 percentage
points rather than 41.5. The nominal increase is 3.42 times as large;
70.8% of that increase disappears when this bracket is held constant
in purchasing power. This is a comparison of estimates, not a causal
attribution of a share of the housing crisis to inflation.

The same-real-income severe burden rate still more than doubles
(2.29 times its 2006 level). Inflation does not erase the problem.
These are repeated cross-sections of residents, not the same households.

REPORT METHODS AND INFLATION CHECK

Report: Boston Foundation/Boston Indicators, 2026 Greater Boston Housing
Report Card, p48, chart on severe renter cost burdens. It identifies ACS
2006-2024 1-year estimates through IPUMS; 2020 is omitted due to experimental
weights. The measure is housing spending above half of household income.
Page 12's population-chart subtitle explicitly defines Greater Boston as
Essex, Middlesex, Norfolk, Suffolk, and Plymouth counties, Massachusetts.
This differs from the Boston-Cambridge-Newton MA-NH MSA. Some other report
figures use that MSA; it must not automatically be substituted here.

I searched the full PDF text, read the relevant rental-housing pages and
endnotes, inspected the rendered p48, and checked the report landing page
and IPUMS monetary-variable documentation. No separate detailed p48
methodology, analysis code, exact PUMA selection, or explicit bracket
inflation base year was located. Chart notes alone do not document those
choices. The geographic target above is the report's five-county definition;
the p48 subtitle itself simply says Greater Boston.

The report DOES discuss inflation elsewhere: p41 uses national CPI-U to
adjust the separate Zillow asking-rent series. That does not establish that
p48's income brackets were adjusted. IPUMS HHINCOME and RENTGRS are in
contemporary dollars unless the user specifically requests an adjustment;
IPUMS attribution does not mean automatic inflation adjustment.

The numerical check strongly supports nominal brackets on p48: the raw
nominal calculation reproduces both rounded endpoints, whereas a fixed
2006 real-income bracket yields about 22% in 2024, and a fixed 2024
real-income bracket yields about 33% in 2006. Neither produces 9% -> 51%.
Thus the evidence indicates that this particular chart leaves its income
brackets nominal. This is an inference from documentation and replication,
not confirmation from the report authors or their original extract settings.

VARIABLES AND ESTIMATOR

Use one row per occupied household from the housing file, TEN in (3,4),
NP > 0 and WGTP > 0. TEN=3 is cash rent; TEN=4 is no cash rent.
Exclude group quarters and vacancies. HINCP is total household money income,
not personal income or family income. GRNTP is monthly gross rent including
utilities/fuels, not contract rent RNTP alone. No-cash-rent households are
retained as having zero rent; positive-income target brackets avoid the
undefined-ratio problem for zero/negative incomes.

Main definition: severe = (12 * GRNTP / HINCP > 0.50), with strict inequality.
Use WGTP, the housing/household survey weight, for both numerator and
denominator. A no-cash-rent household is classified as not burdened.
Main income brackets use raw HINCP, consistent with default IPUMS practice.

Inflation: BLS national CPI-U all items, U.S. city average, not seasonally
adjusted, annual averages 201.6 (2006) and 313.689 (2024).
Factor = 313.689 / 201.6 = 1.5559970238, a 55.6% rise.
Do not multiply income alone when defining a cost ratio in constant dollars:
either leave both rent and income in their own-year units or deflate both.
Here inflation changes which income bracket is selected, not its cost ratio.

Robustness: Census GRPIP is a rounded gross-rent percentage. Using GRPIP>50
instead of the computed ratio yields 9.1% -> 50.8% nominal and 9.1% -> 20.9%
real. Small threshold differences arise from rounding and Census treatment.
Applying the within-survey-year ADJUST (2006) / ADJINC (2024) income factors
and recomputing the ratio gives 9.2% -> 51.1% nominal and 9.2% -> 20.9% real.
Those small factors align rolling income reference periods; they are NOT
the 2006-to-2024 inflation correction. IPUMS advises against relying on a
single uniform within-year factor and does not preapply it by default.
Monthly rent is interview-period spending; IPUMS says it needs no such
within-year adjustment. Both sensitivity families are in comparison.csv.

GEOGRAPHY: WHAT CAN AND CANNOT BE REPRODUCED

2024 uses 2020 PUMAs. Census PUMA-to-county-subdivision relationships show
that the five counties are an exact union of 35 Massachusetts PUMAs.

2006 uses 2000 PUMAs. Exact five-county assignment is impossible from the
public housing file: PUMAs 00400, 02400, 03600 cross into Worcester County;
04100 crosses into Bristol County. The main approximation includes these
whole majority-in-region PUMAs, plus all wholly in-region PUMAs. It excludes
00300 and 04500, which are mostly outside but contain some target towns.
These decisions are made from geography, not selected to optimize a rate.
At Census-2000 populations the approximation includes 117,870 people
outside the target and omits 18,817 inside; these are geographic population
figures, NOT estimated 2006 renter counts. geography.csv records every code.

Alternative 2006 baseline rates for the target income bracket:
- Majority-PUMA approximation: 9.4%.
- Fractional population allocation: 9.5%, assuming each PUMA's renter-income
  burden distribution is spatially uniform within that PUMA; this is a
  model-based approximation, not identification of individual households.
- Only wholly in-region PUMAs: 9.9% (omits parts of target counties).
- All PUMAs touching target counties: 9.0% (adds substantial outside areas).
The latter alternatives change the area; they are specification checks,
not confidence bounds on an exactly identified five-county estimate.

A fully identifiable, fixed two-county check (Essex plus Suffolk) yields
6.6% -> 48.5% nominal but 6.6% -> 17.7% real. It covers a different region,
but confirms the main finding without any cross-boundary allocation.

BROADER CONTEXT

All figures below use gross-rent/income >50%, positive-income renters,
the majority-PUMA approximation, and brackets fixed in 2006 purchasing power.

Income in 2006 dollars        2006    2024    Change (percentage points)
$0 to <$35,000                47.6%   54.6%    +7.0
$35,000 to <$50,000            9.4%   21.5%   +12.1
$50,000 to <$75,000            1.2%    3.6%    +2.4
$75,000 and above              0.3%    0.6%    +0.3

There is real deterioration concentrated below the upper income group,
and the highlighted bracket has the largest increase among these broad
real-income groups. Calling the whole result fabricated would be wrong.

However, using all renters with a valid Census GRPIP (excluding no-cash rent
and undefined percentages), overall severe burden is 25.3% -> 24.8%, while
burden above 30% is 50.4% -> 49.2%. Other denominator choices are supplied
in broader.csv and also show no large aggregate increase. Aggregate trends
combine affordability at each income with changes in the renter income mix;
they cannot show how much the same households' situations improved or
worsened. They do not disprove worsening conditional affordability or prove
that displaced households were replaced by richer ones.

CONCLUSION

The published nominal endpoints are numerically plausible and reproducible
to rounding under a disclosed public-data approximation. They are misleading
as an apples-to-apples measure of changes at the same standard of living.
For the 2006 $35k-$50k income group, the comparable rate rises about 12
percentage points, not 42. The report acknowledges inflation for other
measures and shows broad income-group context; there is no basis here to
assert deliberate cherry-picking or infer the authors' intent.

LIMITATIONS

No original author code or IPUMS extract was available. The 2006 geographic
mismatch prevents an exact five-county replication; a rounded match does
not identify a unique author workflow. Sampling intervals omit geographic
error, misreporting and CPI uncertainty. ACS pretax cash income omits some
noncash supports; reported rent/subsidy treatment can affect measured burden.
No subsidy correction is attempted. Household sizes, composition, quality,
location within the region, migration and tenure selection can change over
time. This is not a causal rent index or a longitudinal household study.
No preregistration was written before exploratory calculations: direct
ratios, GRPIP, income factors, denominators and geography variants were
examined, and all specified variants are retained in the outputs.
